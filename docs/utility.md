# Utility and bot tactics

Grenades are bought with B. The bottom-right inventory shows each carried type, duplicate counts and the equipped grenade. Mouse-wheel cycling includes grenades alongside guns and the knife, skips empty types, and wraps in both directions. Press 4 to cycle carried utility, hold left mouse for an overhand throw, right mouse for underhand, or both for medium strength. Release both buttons to throw. Releasing one button while keeping the other held changes strength on the next simulation step. Quick clicks finish the pin pull before releasing; holding does not cook the fuse. Movement, including falling, adds to launch velocity. Selecting a gun or another grenade, losing pointer lock, opening menus, dying, planting or defusing cancels a pending throw without consuming inventory. Selecting a gun cancels grenade selection. Selection cancels reload, inspection, pending melee and revolver charging. Surviving rounds retain unused utility; death and halftime clear it. Unthrown purchases can be refunded during the current buy window. Purchases from prior rounds cannot be refunded.

The six types are HE, flashbang, smoke, Molotov (T), incendiary (CT), and decoy. Competitive allows four total and two flashes; casual allows three total and one flash. Other types are limited to one, with a shared fire slot. Prices are $300/$200/$300/$400/$500/$50 respectively. These values and the 750 Source units/sec overhand launch parameter are checked against the repository's pinned October 11 2026 shipped snapshot: [weapons.vdata](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-CS2/blob/5278de99318192d682ec7f0e1cc3ea9449f10c6e/game/csgo/pak01_dir/scripts/weapons.vdata), [competitive](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-CS2/blob/5278de99318192d682ec7f0e1cc3ea9449f10c6e/game/csgo/cfg/gamemode_competitive.cfg), and [casual](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-CS2/blob/5278de99318192d682ec7f0e1cc3ea9449f10c6e/game/csgo/cfg/gamemode_casual.cfg). Runtime remains entirely local.

Projectiles simulate on the fixed game clock, sweep their travel against native map triangles and the previously discarded native `csgo_grenadeclip` surfaces, bounce with energy loss, and detonate after their fuse or fire-grenade ground impact. Smoke and decoy detonation requires a confirmed resting ground contact, so low speed near a flight apex does not trigger an airborne bloom. Settled grenades sleep until detonation; lost projectiles are discarded after 30 seconds rather than detonating in midair. HE has distance falloff, cover rejection, armor and mode-specific friendly damage. Flash strength depends on distance, facing and clear sight; the player sees a fading white overlay and blinded bots cannot acquire or shoot. Smoke blocks bot perception. Bullet paths briefly remove intersected cloud cells and HE temporarily clears nearby smoke only when the blast has clear sight. Smoke extinguishes nearby fire with a cover check. Fire uses the shipped 40 damage/second rate at 0.2-second intervals, remains on nearby receiving-floor cells, and ignores armor. Fractional simulation-tick remainder is retained so the cadence does not slow down at 128 Hz. Decoys emit spatial gunfire and misleading last-known-position reports, then detonate with a small nearby blast at expiry. Utility deaths use normal round/death/drop handling, with the actual grenade name in the kill feed.

Bots spend their available cash on role-based grenade combinations, use only observed or teammate-reported enemy positions, avoid throwing damage utility onto nearby teammates, stagger team throws, smoke retreat/retake lanes, flash lost contacts, and throw damage utility at visible contacts. They flee nearby fire, coordinate short entry windows after friendly flashes, trade a nearby fallen teammate using that teammate's last contact, and save when travel plus remaining defuse time cannot beat the known bomb deadline. Takeover transfers the bot's unused utility and remaining flash blindness. Smoke and flash affect their existing perception/combat rather than only rendering a visual effect.

These are authored browser reconstructions, not an extracted Valve grenade simulation. Collision uses swept point travel with a small surface clearance rather than a full native grenade hull. HE/flash/settling fuse behavior, bounce elasticity, strength-to-launch interpolation, HE falloff, flash exposure and fire propagation remain reconstructions. The fire-grenade two-second fuse and maximum 30-degree impact slope are imported shipped parameters. Smoke uses wall-constrained instanced cells with analytic ray/sphere optical depth, up to four procedural density samples per fragment, drifting turbulence and an authored top-light tint. Rays also work from inside a cell; bullet and HE openings refill continuously over 0.35 seconds, with the same density recovery controlling bot visibility. This remains a coarse-cell approximation, without Source 2's voxel fluid solver, scene-dependent volumetric lighting or exact bullet channels. Smoke optical depth now ends at actual opaque/cutout scene geometry from a depth prepass, including when the camera is inside a cell. This improves wall, floor and crate intersections without adding a native fluid solver. Fire uses procedural local meshes. All six grenade bodies now use native Valve meshes, materials and skeletal attachments extracted from pinned Steam depot manifest `4758919807587010422`, both in first person and in flight. Regenerate with `python3 scripts/import-grenades.py`; source resources and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `packages/assets/public/models/grenades/source.json`. The importer uses the existing Source2Viewer, DepotDownloader and WebP packing pipeline, leaves original VPKs intact, and bundles every runtime texture locally. First-person utility retargets the existing native hands to 48 pinned first-person animation clips: idle, draw, pin pull, high/mid/low charge, and overhand/underhand throws for each grenade. Regenerate clips with `python3 scripts/import-grenade-clips.py` (cached Steam archive 473); per-clip durations, source hashes and sound events are recorded in `packages/assets/public/models/grenades/clips/source.json`. Pin readiness and release recovery follow the native clip lengths. The native animation graph blending and precise gameplay release event remain reconstructed; no native lineup or timing equivalence is claimed. Decoy uses the shared default grenade clips. Native per-grenade draw, pin, throw, bounce and effect sounds are now bundled through the pinned CS2 audio importer (shared native cues are used where appropriate). No claim of native lineup equivalence is made.

Focused checks: `bun run test src/utility.test.ts` and `bun run test:browser tests/utility.spec.ts`. The browser check covers actual buying/refunding, pointer-lock throw input, detonation, smoke occlusion, flash exposure, and no external requests or browser errors; screenshots are written to ignored `artifacts/`.

## Performance boundary

Smoke and fire each use one instanced draw per effect. Fire shares the controller's sphere geometry; smoke clones the small sphere buffer to own its per-instance density attribute, and disposes that clone on expiry. Physics/timers remain fixed-step; fire animation, opacity and the flash overlay update only when rendering. Static smoke instance matrices do not change as the timer advances. Bullet holes use expiry timestamps; only changing density uploads the small instance-density buffer, with no per-frame geometry or texture allocation. Each smoke fragment has at most four density samples, reduced to two on low quality or with five to eight visible clouds, and one with nine or more clouds; no volume textures are allocated. Visible smoke uses a cached unlit scene-depth prepass, with its far plane bounded to the furthest visible cloud. Its depth target uses five bytes per pixel (R8 placeholder color plus 32-bit depth), at the drawing-buffer resolution on high quality and half each dimension on low. Low-resolution nearest depth sampling can quantize cover edges by roughly one display pixel. The target is released when no smoke intersects the camera frustum. The original world color pass retains native material tone mapping and contrast order; cached depth-material variants preserve cutouts, side, displacement and clipping. Renderer target, viewport, camera layers and shadow-update state are restored after compositing. Projectile motion and damage samples reuse scratch vectors. The controller caps flying projectiles at 32 and effects at 48, removing scene objects and disposing per-effect materials/instance buffers on expiration/reset without destroying shared geometry. Bot utility decisions run at 4 Hz and fire damage at 5 Hz.

`UTILITY_BENCH_LABEL=after bun run test:browser tests/utility-performance.spec.ts` measures this component, not overall rendering FPS. An October 13 local Chromium before/after sample used 12 live smoke clouds (1,159 cells), 64 settled dropped guns, one simulated second of warmup, then 384 fixed ticks and 192 render-state updates. CPU time for those three simulated seconds fell from 8.0 ms to 0.6 ms; static instance-buffer upload-version changes fell from 4,608 to zero. Settled guns now sleep, so they no longer repeat gravity/support queries. These are one-run component timings, not a claim about whole-game frame rate, GPU smoke overdraw or other machines. Raw results are ignored artifacts `utility-performance-before.json` and `utility-performance-after.json`.

`apps/game/tests/utility-bots.spec.ts` also exercises real bot navigation consuming a scheduled flash entry, recent-contact trade routing, reachable versus impossible defuse deadlines, and decoy expiry damage.

An additional October 13 isolated Chromium smoke-renderer fixture at 1000×700 measured 111 cells: one extra draw and 43,956 triangles. Median frame intervals were 16.7 ms clear and 16.6 ms with smoke (p95 17.5/17.6 ms, 39 measured frames each). This is a local vsync-limited sample, not a GPU cost measurement or low-end guarantee. Outside, inside, bullet openings and blast clearing were visually inspected; the fixture made no external requests and emitted no browser or shader errors.

After the density renderer change, the same 12-cloud component fixture measured 3.5 ms for 384 fixed ticks and 192 render updates (0.0091 ms per tick), with 1,159 cells, 64 drops and zero static matrix uploads. This replaces the older renderer's 0.6 ms sample above; density recovery adds a bounded per-cell scan during rendering.

A deliberate 12-cloud overlap stress fixture (1,332 cells at 1000×700) exposed expensive inside-camera overdraw: four samples measured 44.7 ms median / 49.2 ms p95 frame intervals. The adaptive one-sample budget reduced that run to 16.6 / 17.6 ms, preserving analytic optical depth while reducing procedural detail. Outside the overlapping clouds both runs remained near 16.7 ms. These are local Chromium frame intervals, not hardware-independent GPU timings.

## Verified utility parameters and remaining unknowns

`python3 scripts/import-utility-data.py` imports `apps/game/src/data/utility.json` from the existing pinned GameTracking-CS2 commit `5278de99318192d682ec7f0e1cc3ea9449f10c6e`. It records source URLs and SHA-256 hashes for [the convar dump](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-CS2/blob/5278de99318192d682ec7f0e1cc3ea9449f10c6e/DumpSource2/convars.txt), `weapons.vdata` and both mode configuration files. Runtime requires no network access.

The previous shared 40% teammate-utility reduction did not match the shipped grenade coefficient: competitive explicitly sets `ff_damage_reduction_grenade=0.85`, while `ff_damage_reduction_other=0.4`. The browser currently classifies HE/decoy as blast and inferno as other; the configuration values do not prove native inferno damage-flag dispatch. That classification remains a reconstruction. Casual sets teammate blast and self-blast reduction to zero; competitive retains full self-blast damage. Decoy detonation no longer damages teammates, following `ff_damage_decoy_explosion=false` and its explicit team-damage description. Self-decoy dispatch remains the existing self-blast reconstruction. Zero damage now also skips the damage flash and equipment invalidation. Inferno self damage is unchanged; whether the native self-grenade coefficient applies to inferno remains unverified. The pinned `inferno_friendly_fire_duration=6` description says FF is credited back to the thrower for that period; it does not establish a six-second damage immunity cutoff, so none is invented here.

Inferno configuration explicitly describes 40 damage per second for both factions, a 0.2-second damage interval, 16 maximum flame nodes, 42 Source units minimum node spacing, and maximum spread distances of 150 units for Molotovs and 110 for incendiaries (3.81 and 2.794 meters). The browser previously ramped damage beyond that declared rate and generated more nodes than the configured maximum. Its floor-checked grid now respects those constraints and fills outward from the ignition point. This is **not** Valve's flame branching, spawn scheduling or per-flame lifetime solver: all browser nodes still appear together and share the effect lifetime. Selecting the nearest 16 grid cells usually yields a smaller footprint than the maximum spread radius; the native maximum is a bound, not a reproduced growth envelope. Seven- and 5.5-second lifetimes are native average flame-lifetime inputs, not proof that the whole native inferno expires on that exact clock.

The weapon file also contains HE damage, armor-ratio and range fields. They are retained in the data snapshot but are deliberately not substituted into the existing blast equations: a field's presence does not prove how native explosion damage dispatch combines it with armor, cover and distance. No runnable CS2 comparison was available for this work. Full collision hulls, bounce/material response, throw-angle correction, exact release-gameplay scheduling, explosion sampling, flash exposure and the fire-spread algorithm remain unverified. Existing approximations are identified here rather than presented as extracted native behavior.

`apps/game/tests/utility-rules.spec.ts` exercises the actual runtime damage path: casual/competitive self/team blast scaling, disabled decoy teammate damage, exactly 40/120 fire damage after one/three fixed-step seconds, and native-count/spacing constraints on generated fire. Unit cases cover the 30-degree impact boundary and faction spread limits.

### Scene-depth validation and rendering cost

`bun run test:browser tests/smoke-depth.spec.ts` exercises foreground cover, intersections from outside and inside a cell, cutout openings, original material restoration, half-resolution depth, fractional device-pixel-ratio viewport alignment, destination render-target viewport restoration and target release. Native Dust II screenshots were also inspected at high and low quality; the cloud obscures the A-site floor and intersects nearby crates while the foreground viewmodel remains clear. No browser/shader errors or external asset requests were observed.

An October 13 Chromium sample at a 1440×900 CSS viewport used one 85-cell A-site cloud. Each configuration measured 15 synchronous renders after three warmups with `gl.finish()`: these are local CPU-plus-GPU completion wall times, not GPU timer-query measurements or gameplay FPS. High quality measured no smoke at 4.7 ms median / 5.3 ms p95, the prior surface-only smoke at 4.5 / 5.4 ms, and scene-depth smoke at 8.2 / 9.1 ms. Low quality measured 4.4 / 5.1 ms, 4.3 / 4.9 ms and 8.2 / 9.7 ms respectively. Low resolution reduced target memory, but did not materially reduce the geometry-dominated cost in this sample.

Depth adds 207 draws beyond the surface-only smoke (656 → 863) and raises submitted triangles from 2,070,557 to 3,455,719 in this view. Limiting the depth camera to visible cloud distance reduced an earlier unrestricted prepass's 633 extra draws to 207. The high target was 1440×900 (6,480,000 bytes); low was 607×379 (1,150,265 bytes), following the game's reduced drawing-buffer scale. These estimates exclude driver allocation overhead. Both targets released their two textures when smoke disappeared. A zero-density smoke comparison preserved the native world's color output within two 8-bit channel levels (one differing channel on high, 28 on low), including the fractional-DPR viewport regression. This is a fidelity improvement with measurable extra rendering cost, not a performance improvement over surface-only smoke.

Raw results and screenshots are ignored artifacts: `artifacts/smoke-native-depth-high.json`, `artifacts/smoke-native-depth-low.json`, `artifacts/smoke-depth-native-high.png`, `artifacts/smoke-depth-native-low.png`, `artifacts/smoke-depth-near-wall.png` and `artifacts/smoke-depth-distant-wall.png`. The procedural density, authored lighting, coarse cells and replenishment remain browser reconstructions; pinned shipped files do not establish Source 2 fluid or lighting parity, and no runnable native CS2 comparison was available.

HE, flash and decoy blasts play at full effects volume with longer-range spatial attenuation. Blasts take priority over footsteps within the bounded 16-voice effects pool. HE flashes peak immediately and expand/fade over their short lifetime. These presentation cues remain browser reconstructions using native CS2 recordings.
